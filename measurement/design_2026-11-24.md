## 1. Intent and why

The fixture is built, both boards are mounted, and nothing acoustic has been
measured. The host model predicts a narrow broadside beam and a point null at
54.75°, but the prediction is ideal. This campaign produces the first real
numbers: whether the boards are phase-locked, whether the 54.75° lobes are
suppressed, how wide the main beam is, and how much energy leaks above 60°.
The output is a procedure and a data log that can be repeated after any change
to the fixture, firmware, or drive level.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**

1. Prove the two boards are phase-locked from one sync source (scope only).
2. Measure all five acoustic acceptance criteria in `fpga/PLAN.md` §1 in
   relative dB.
3. Find and record the deepest 54.75° null and the trim that produces it.
4. Measure main-beam width and the >60° leak at two distances.
5. Leave a repeatable procedure, a BOM, and a machine-readable data log.

**Non-goals**

- Absolute SPL (dB re 20 µPa) until the meeting decides it is needed; the
  design stores raw volts so it can be converted later.
- An automated turntable or a fully scripted rig. The user chose manual
  mechanics with SCPI readout where available.
- Far-field measurements beyond 4 m; the combined aperture needs ~13–26 m.
- VGGT or ArUco metrology. VGGT cannot place a 2 cm receiver to ±5 mm
  (scale ambiguity, no reliable segmentation) and ArUco is unnecessary once
  the rotation stage is the angle reference.
- A calibrated measurement microphone. Every criterion is relative.

**Anti-goals**

- A number that cannot distinguish near-field blur from a geometry error.
- Any measurement taken before both boards are reflashed to matching firmware.
- Sustained streaming on the CH340 adapter (known-bad Windows driver).

## 3. Verified constraints and findings

### 3.1 Hardware and firmware state

- Two 16×16 boards, 512 transducers, 10.5 mm pitch, 40 kHz, λ = 8.575 mm at
  343 m/s. 16 phase bins (22.5°), 8 duty levels, 38.4 kBaud, 258-byte frames.
- Mounted at 15.750 mm seam, 5.250 mm y offset, 4.848 mm PCB edge gap;
  combined aperture 330.75 × 162.75 mm. Element-centre coordinates in the
  fixture base frame run x = 25.406…356.156 mm, y = 33.706…196.456 mm; the
  acoustic centre is **(190.78, 115.08) mm** in that frame.
- Secondary trim: 20 ns per step, 0–255 = 5.1 µs = 73.4° at 40 kHz. On a
  shared bus the value byte is also seen by the primary, so
  `150, 151, 192, 193, 200–203, 253` must be skipped. **With the two-port
  `BoardLink` this does not apply**: `send_trim` writes only to the secondary,
  so the full 0–255 range is safe.
- **Both boards still hold pre-2026-11-13 firmware.** The EPCS16 images are
  the 2026-11-13 builds; the USB-Blaster is expected in hand. Reflash is
  step 0 and gates everything else.

### 3.2 The null is a point, and the near field matters

The 5.25 mm half-pitch offset cancels the two boards' fields exactly at
54.75° in the far field; 1 mm of seam/offset error raises the null to about
−23 dB, 2 mm to about −18 dB. The null therefore measures mechanical geometry
as much as phase lock. The ±0.15 mm fixture target corresponds to about
−27 dB.

An exact near-field sum over the 512 measured positions (spherical phases,
per-element circular-piston directivity) shows that the null is shallow at
bench distances but usable from about 2 m:

| distance | 9 mm piston | 10 mm piston | isotropic |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.0 m | −20.8 dB | −24.3 dB | −14.0 dB |
| 1.5 m | −25.2 dB | −26.9 dB | −18.9 dB |
| 2.0 m | −28.9 dB | −29.8 dB | −23.8 dB |
| 2.5 m | −31.5 dB | −32.1 dB | −27.5 dB |
| 3.0 m | −33.7 dB | −34.0 dB | −30.4 dB |
| 4.0 m | −38.5 dB | −38.6 dB | −34.9 dB |

Level at 54.75° relative to the **polar-cut maximum** at the same distance,
all phases zero. The cut maximum is 1.5–5.8° off axis at 1–2.5 m and on axis
at ≥3 m; it must be found, not assumed. The isotropic model is misleading and
must not be used to judge distances.

The element diameter is uncertain: the housing OD is ~9.9 mm, the active
radiator is smaller. The Murata −6 dB full-angle spec (~80°) lies between a
9 mm piston (84°) and a 10 mm piston (74°), so **9.5 mm is the nominal model
value**, and the diameter is a fit parameter from the data
(`--element-diameter-mm`). At 2.5–3 m the 9 vs 10 mm difference is only
0.5–1.5 dB; at 1 m it is 3.5 dB.

The main-beam −3 dB width is 1.31° across x and 2.67° across y in the far
field, so a 0.25° angular grid is needed near 0° and around 48–62°; a 0.5–1°
protractor alone is not enough to find the peak.

### 3.3 Drive level and air nonlinearity

If the transducers are driven at 5–10 Vpp, 512 coherent elements at 2–3 m
produce roughly 145–149 dB SPL on the main beam (using the Murata 120 dB at
0.3 m / 10 Vrms figure). At that level the 40 kHz fundamental compresses in
air while the −30 dB sidelobes propagate linearly, so **relative sidelobe and
null levels read worse than the linear model**. The actual drive voltage is
unknown and must be measured at a transducer pad. A duty-step linearity test
(width 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 = 0, −3.0, −8.3, −14.2 dB) separates air saturation from
receiver nonlinearity. The acceptance measurement is taken at the lowest drive
that still gives adequate SNR, with the full-drive value recorded separately.

### 3.4 The 200 Hz mode and gating

`music_player.vhd` makes the 200 Hz mode a **50 % square gate: 2.5 ms on,
2.5 ms off, 5 ms period**. The aperture fill time at 54.75° is
0.331 × sin 54.75° / 343 = **0.787 ms**; transducer Q ≈ 15–20 adds about
1 ms of settling. A 2.5 ms burst therefore has only ~0.7 ms of usable steady
state. That is enough for a 0.4–0.7 ms analysis slice, but the **5 ms period
is the real problem**: the room is re-excited every 5 ms, so a 50 % duty
never lets the reverberant field decay. In a normal room the null will be
reverb-limited, not geometry-limited.

Two options:

1. **No firmware change:** use the existing 2.5 ms burst from a silent start.
   The steady-state field begins at about +1.5 ms after the first arrival
   (0.787 ms aperture fill plus ~0.7 ms cascade settling), so the usable
   window is only 1.6–2.4 ms. The room is silent before the burst only if the
   pattern frame is latched while the array is muted, which is not possible on
   a shared bus without a frame strobe. This works outdoors (no walls, only
   the ground bounce) and is marginal in the lab.
2. **Measurement gate mode (recommended):** add a ~10 Hz gate
   (**4 ms on, 100 ms period**) to both boards in the pending reflash. The
   settled window is then 1.6–4.0 ms after the first arrival — about 2.4 ms,
   ~96 carrier cycles. At 3 m the first floor bounce is +5.07 ms
   (h = 1.6 m) and at 4 m +3.27 ms, so the array and mic must be at
   **height ≥ 1.6 m**; a 1.2 m height puts the floor bounce at +2.45 ms and
   eats most of the window. At 40 kHz air absorption (~1.2–1.3 dB/m) gives
   RT60 ≈ 0.07–0.09 s, so 100 ms decays ~90 dB — the period is sufficient.
   Early side-wall/ceiling reflections that fall *inside* the window are a
   separate problem: map them with a single burst (§5.4) and use a foam
   screen if needed.
   Because the campaign keeps **two UART ports**, the gate is **wired**:
   the primary generates it on `MISC_C` (PIN_67), the secondary takes it on
   `MISC_F` (PIN_115), and the mode byte only selects the mode. The wire
   also serves as the scope Ch2 trigger, and it makes the millisecond UART
   skew irrelevant. Both boards are reflashed with the gate build (primary
   output + secondary input); this is the campaign's one firmware change,
   roughly a day including the testbench and fit. The boards need reflashing
   anyway, so this is the cheapest moment to add it.

**Recommendation:** option 2. If the user wants the fastest first data,
option 1 outdoors is the fallback.

### 3.5 Receiver

- A 40 kHz piezo transducer (MA40S4R preferred, MA40S4S spare) into a
  battery-powered buffer. The receiver's own directivity cancels because the
  array rotates about its centre while the mic stays fixed and aimed at the
  array centre; all sound arrives along the mic axis.
- Expected level: the Murata receive sensitivity is quoted around −63 dB with
  an ambiguous reference (1 V/Pa vs 10 V/Pa), so the main beam can be
  0.2–3.6 Vrms and a −30 dB null 10–110 mV. Use a **switchable 0 dB / +20 dB**
  stage, never 40–60 dB (it would clip the main beam). Measure the actual
  level before fixing the gain.
- MEMS and electret capsules (SPH0641LU4H-1, WM-61A) clip near 120 dB SPL and
  are rejected for the main-beam measurement; the piezo is passive and
  survives.
- The receiver sits in a grounded metal box with the transducer face flush
  and foam around the sides; ≤1.5 m of coax, 1× probe setting. A 10× probe
  loses 20 dB of SNR.
- **EMI block test before every session:** hold a book or dense foam 5 cm in
  front of the mic; the 40 kHz signal must drop ≥45 dB. Whatever remains is
  the coherent EMI floor from the board's several-amp 40 kHz drive.

### 3.6 Electro-mechanical matching

- Element scatter and a few-percent board-to-board gain mismatch set a
  practical null floor of roughly −25 to −35 dB. Measure board A alone and
  board B alone at 0° and record the amplitude ratio; match supplies to
  <20 mV or use one supply.
- Temperature moves the grating angle: 340/343/346 m/s gives
  54.05/54.75/55.47°. A 5 °C change moves the null ~0.7°. Log temperature and
  humidity every run.
- The 3 mm B-left datum fence sits in the seam gap; compare φ = 0/180 with
  φ = 90/270 to detect a fence diffraction spike near 60–90°.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Receiver and digitisation

- **Transducer:** MA40S4R ×2 (one spare). Flush in a small grounded box;
  acoustic port at a marked point.
- **Buffer:** JFET source follower (2SK209 or BF862) with a 1 MΩ gate resistor,
  or a non-inverting OPA2134/TL072 with a 1 MΩ bias and a switchable +20 dB
  (Rf/Rg = 9.1 kΩ / 1 kΩ). Powered by two 9 V batteries (±9 V) or one 9 V
  with a 4.5 V bias. Output AC-coupled into the scope at 1 MΩ.
- **Digitisation:** scope 4-channel, 2 GS/s (confirm the model and memory).
  Acoustic capture at 1–10 MS/s, 20–30 ms record; compute a Hann-windowed
  DFT/IQ amplitude at 40 kHz inside the gate window. For the sync check use
  two captures: a **20 ms capture at ~100 MS/s** (2 Mpts) to count pulses and
  a **50 µs window at 2 GS/s** for the 195 ns pulse shape. A 20 ms record at
  2 GS/s would need 40 Mpts per channel and is not required. Never read Vpp
  below 100 mV; use the FFT/gated RMS.
- **Channel plan:** Ch1 mic, Ch2 `MISC_C` gate, Ch3 primary `MISC_D`,
  Ch4 secondary `MISC_A`.
- **Verification:** noise floor with the array off; EMI block test; duty-step
  linearity at 0°.

### 5.2 Mechanics, pointing, localisation

- Stand the fixture with the **seam line vertical** (the base plane vertical,
  y-axis vertical) so the az 0/180 lobes are horizontal at the mic height.
  Roll the fixture 90° about its normal for the φ = 90/270 runs.
- Rotation stage about the **acoustic centre (190.78, 115.08) mm** in the base
  frame; align with a plumb line. A plywood backer on a lab jack or a large
  printed turntable with a tangent screw; the tangent arm needs a fine screw
  (0.1° steps).
- Mic on an independent stand at 2.5–3.0 m, at the array-centre height
  (±5 mm), with **array centre and mic at ≥1.6 m** so the first floor bounce
  arrives at +5.07 ms (3 m) or +3.27 ms (4 m), outside the settled window.
  Laser distance meter for range; laser pointer along the array normal onto
  a mm tape at 4 m for the 0° reference (1 mm = 0.014°); printed 360°
  protractor for the coarse angle; tangent screw for the fine scan.
- Record the mic coordinates in the array frame, the stage angle, and the
  distance. Do not rely on the protractor alone near 0° (1.31° HPBW).
- The mic is never moved during a polar cut; the array rotates.

### 5.3 Scope-only sync check

1. Reflash both boards (SFL first, then the JIC) and record the build IDs.
2. Probe primary `MISC_D` (PIN_120) on Ch3 and secondary `MISC_A` (PIN_65) on
   Ch4; common ground. Trigger on Ch3 rising, 1.5 V.
3. 5 µs/div: one ~195 ns pulse every 25 µs, no missing or doubled edges over
   a 20 ms capture. 100 ns/div: pulse width, rise time, amplitude.
4. Skew: 20 ns/div, trigger on Ch4, average 64; record the Ch3–Ch4 delay.
5. Trim: send `0xE0 0x5A N` for N = 0, 32, 64, …, 255. All values are safe
   on two ports (the shared-bus skip list does not apply). The secondary pulse
   must move monotonically by ~5.1 µs over the full range (~160 ns per
   8 steps). A flat or jumping curve means the trim command or firmware is
   wrong.
6. "Locked" means one secondary pulse per primary pulse, a fixed delay with
   <100 ns jitter, no slips over the long capture. A drifting or missing
   pulse means the secondary is free-running; stop and fix before acoustics.

### 5.4 Gating and capture

**Recommended firmware gate (option 2):** 10 Hz, **4 ms on, 100 ms period**.
The primary generates it on `MISC_C`; the secondary gates its drive from
`MISC_F`; the mode byte only selects the mode. `MISC_C` is also scope Ch2.
The settled analysis window is **1.6–4.0 ms after the first arrival**
(0.787 ms aperture fill plus ~0.7 ms cascade settling), about 2.4 ms /
96 cycles. At 3 m with h ≥ 1.6 m the floor bounce is +5.07 ms; at 4 m it is
+3.27 ms, so the window at 4 m ends at 3.2 ms. Do not start the window at
0.9 ms: the far elements have not settled yet.

**Echo map (before every session):** fire one burst at 0° and capture 30 ms.
Any reflection above −40 dB inside the settled window is a room problem;
raise the mic/array, move to a larger space, or add a foam screen.

**Host sequence (wired gate, two ports):**

1. Send an all-off frame to both boards; wait 200 ms.
2. Send the gate-mode byte (0xCC) to both ports. The primary starts gating;
   the secondary follows `MISC_F`. The array is still muted.
3. Send the pattern frames to both boards in parallel.
4. Wait 200–300 ms. Any emission during the frame latch is then >200 ms old
   and fully decayed.
5. Trigger the scope on Ch2 (`MISC_C` gate, 3.3 V); capture 30 ms at
   1 MS/s. In the null do not trigger on the mic: a reflection can be louder
   than the direct arrival.
6. Locate the first arrival, gate 1.6–4.0 ms after it, FFT at 40 kHz, record
   volts and dB.

For the no-firmware fallback (outdoors), use the existing 2.5 ms burst and
the mic trigger on the first burst after silence.

Outdoors, the same sequence with the existing 2.5 ms burst and a mic trigger
on the first burst after silence is the no-firmware fallback.

### 5.5 Null search, polar cuts, metrics

**Order:**

1. Sync check (5.3).
2. Board balance at 0°/broadside: A-only, B-only, both; record V_A/V_B and
   the true 0° peak from a ±3° scan at 0.25° steps.
3. Coarse null hunt at +54.75°: scan 50–60° in 0.5° steps at trim 127.
4. Trim sweep 0–255 step 16 at the local minimum; skip the shared-bus unsafe
   values.
5. The **±54.75° diagnostic**: repeat the trim sweep at −54.75° (φ = 180).
   - Both minima at the same trim (|T0 − T180| ≤ 8): seam within ~0.03 mm.
   - Minima at different trims: a physical seam error; the required trim
     differs by 2 × 34.3° per mm, and 1 trim step = 0.0084 mm of seam. The
     true electrical-zero trim is (T0 + T180)/2; tuning only one side makes
     the other worse.
6. Fine scan: lock the best trim, scan 48–62° in 0.25–0.5° steps; iterate the
   angle and trim once.
7. If the null is shallow, run the dissecting checks in §5.7.
8. Polar cuts at φ = 0/90/45: 0.25° steps from −5 to +5°, 2° from 5 to 48°,
   0.5° from 48 to 62°, 3° from 62 to 90°; ≥1 s dwell (10 bursts at 10 Hz).
9. Repeat at a second distance (2 m and 3 m, or 3 m and 4 m).
10. Repeat the 54.75° point after 30–60 min warm-up.

**Report three numbers per cut:** the exact 54.75° (or measured θ_null) depth;
the 50–60° band peak (the split lobes); the >60° peak (the acceptance metric).
Normalise to the measured true 0° peak, not the nominal 0°.

**Configuration matrix.** The pattern cuts use plane-wave phases (all zero
for broadside, `focus_z = null`). The 54.75° null is a plane-wave interference
condition; focusing at the mic would steer the beam onto the null direction
and erase what we are measuring. Focused runs (`--focus-z`) are a separate
"listener at 0°" condition and are not used for the acceptance pattern.
Acceptance is reported for the best delivered configuration: uniform + trim
is the baseline; Hann and the optimizer are the improvement. Full polar cuts
at uniform at both distances; one cut each at Hann and optimized at the
primary distance, to show the gain/leak trade.

### 5.7 Failure-mode diagnostics

| Symptom | Fastest separating check |
|---|---|
| No trim dip at all | Sync (5.3), firmware IDs, board select, trim decode. |
| T0 ≠ T180 (minima at different trims) | Physical seam error; 1 step = 0.0084 mm. |
| Same trim, shallow null | A-only/B-only balance; then distance (2 m → 3 m; deepens 4–8 dB if near-field). |
| Null at the wrong angle | 48–62° fine scan; temperature/sound-speed. |
| Amplitude does not follow duty steps | Air saturation or receiver nonlinearity; reduce drive. |
| Level survives a book in front of the mic | Coherent 40 kHz EMI; shield, move cables. |
| Null drifts over 30–60 min | Thermal/supply asymmetry; match supplies, log temperature. |

## 6. Sequence and deliverables

1. **Step 0:** reflash both boards with the gate build (primary `MISC_C`
   output, secondary `MISC_F` input; SFL then JIC). Then verify the
   **secondary correction table** (raw vs permuted) with
   `table_test.py`/`wire_probe.py` at the driver pads:
   `fpga/secondary/README.md` says that A/B was only done on the primary. A
   wrong orientation puts roughly half of board B 180° out, drops the 0° sum,
   and fills the null while looking like a geometry error.
2. **Step 1:** sync check, reported with scope settings and screenshots.
3. **Step 2:** receiver build and verification (noise, EMI, linearity).
4. **Step 3:** mechanical setup and geometry log.
5. **Step 4:** board balance; broadside reference; null hunt; ±54.75°
   diagnostic; polar cuts; second distance; drift repeat.
6. **Step 5:** data log, plots, and a short report.

**Criterion 5 (atomic frame update).** For static beams `fpga/PLAN.md` §1
allows the residual transient to be documented and accepted. The shared-bus
update is sequential (A, A, B, B, ~270 ms), so the campaign documents the
transient and does not add the `MISC_C`→`MISC_F` strobe; `MISC_C` is used for
the gate trigger. If atomic swapping becomes a requirement, the strobe
returns and the gate trigger moves to another pin.

Deliverables: this design/plan; the sync-check log; the receiver BOM and
schematic; after approval, a small `measurement/` acquisition helper (SCPI +
serial, hardware-free tests) and the near-field predictor; the first data log.

## 7. Decisions

**Decided 2026-11-24:**

1. **Gate:** yes, one flash with a 4 ms / 10 Hz gate. Because two UART ports
   are kept, the gate is **wired**: primary `MISC_C` (PIN_67) → secondary
   `MISC_F` (PIN_115); the mode byte only selects the mode. The wire is also
   scope Ch2.
2. **Serial:** two ports (primary CH340, secondary FTDI). The wired gate makes
   the port skew irrelevant. Verify the CH341 driver is not the 3.9.2024.9
   build; keep writes short; no hot-plugging during a capture.
3. **Secondary table:** not verified; the driver-pad check is step 0.
4. **SCPI:** unknown; the helper is written with a manual fallback, and the
   scope model is confirmed before the helper is finalised.

**Still open:**

5. **Absolute SPL** — the user will ask at the meeting; raw volts are stored.
6. **Scope model and memory** — needed for the exact sync capture and the
   helper.

**Settled by this design (the user can override):**

7. **Drive voltage** — measured at a transducer pad in step 0; it fixes the
   SPL estimate and the receiver gain.
8. **Acceptance configuration** — report uniform + trim, Hann, and optimized;
   acceptance is the best delivered configuration, with uniform as the
   baseline.
9. **Criterion 5** — document the non-atomic static update and accept it; no
   frame strobe in this campaign.

## 8. Risks

- Reflash fails or the Blaster is delayed: everything waits.
- The lab's 5 ms gate reverb limits the null; the gate mode or the outdoor
  fallback is needed.
- A 3–4 dB element-directivity model error at short distances: measure at
  ≥2.5 m and fit the diameter.
- Air saturation at full drive compresses the main beam: run the duty-step
  test, use the lowest adequate drive.
- The CH340 driver: do not use it for sustained streaming; prefer the FTDI.
- The array holds its last frame: send an OFF frame before moving the mic.

Adopted: the duty-step linearity test; the ±54.75° trim diagnostic; the
A-only/B-only balance; the 0.787 ms aperture fill and the 2.5 ms burst's
~0.7 ms steady state; the piezo-versus-MEMS clipping argument; the 0/+20 dB
gain range; the EMI block test; the laser-pointer pointing (1 mm at 4 m =
0.014°); the three-number reporting; the non-uniform angular sampling; the
shared-supply/amplitude-match point; temperature logging; the R/R+λ/4 ripple
check for CW.

Also adopted from the design review: the **secondary correction-table A/B**
before acoustics — `fpga/secondary/README.md` says the raw-vs-permuted check
was only done on the primary; and the **1.6 ms start / 4 ms gate** correction
(the original 0.9–2.0 ms window began before the far elements settled).

Corrected: the exact near-field sum with a 9 mm piston gives −20.8 dB at 1 m
and −33.7 dB at 3 m (not −24/−34 for 10 mm); the 9-vs-10 mm correction is
3.5 dB at 1 m but only 0.5–1.5 dB at 2.5–3 m. The Fresnel estimate of a
3.3 dB on-axis depression at 3 m is not what the exact sum gives; the exact
sum has the cut maximum on axis at ≥3 m. The 90° "−29 dB rigid-baffle Bessel
dip" is not present for a 9–10 mm piston at 40 kHz; the qualitative can
shadowing point is plausible and should be measured, not modelled as a dip.

The reviewer's "70 dB in 100 ms implies RT60 0.09 s; a normal lab is
0.3–0.8 s" uses a mid-frequency RT60. At 40 kHz air absorption
(~1.2–1.3 dB/m) gives RT60 ≈ 0.07–0.09 s, so 100 ms decays ~90 dB and the
gate period is sufficient. Early reflections *inside* the settled window are
still a real risk; the echo map, the ≥1.6 m height, and a foam screen address
them, not the period.

Rejected: "no USB-Blaster on hand" — the user has arranged one; reflash is
step 0. "Use a 40–60 dB preamp" — it would clip the main beam; 0/+20 dB is
correct. "Only a 1/4-inch condenser survives" — a piezo receiver also
survives and is cheaper; the condenser is an absolute-SPL option, not a
relative one.
