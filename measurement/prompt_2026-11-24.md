## Verified hardware state (2026-11-24)

- Two identical 16×16 SonicSurface boards (256 transducers each, 512 total),
  EP4CE6 core board, 40 kHz carrier, 16 phase bins, 38.4 kBaud UART.
- Mounted coplanar in the acrylic fixture: **element-centre seam 15.750 mm**,
  **y offset 5.250 mm**, PCB edge gap 4.848 mm, 30 mm ground clearance.
  Each board rotated 90° CCW; element lattice 10.5 mm.
- **What is actually flashed (verified from the session history, 2026-11-24):**
  the primary's last flash was **2026-11-12 21:42** (duty-weighting image,
  `2b03b89`). Its **`MISC_D` (PIN_120) is the old 40 kHz square wave
  (`COUNT[6]`), 0→2.5 V, 50 % duty** — *not* the one-clock pulse. That pulse
  was added 2026-11-13 18:28 (`e50bfac`) and the 11-13 image was never flashed.
  The secondary was flashed 2026-11-18 (secondary `SyncTrim`, commit 32cf5b1)
  but now holds **unknown firmware** per the user.
- **No USB-Blaster is available as of 2026-11-24**, so neither board can be
  reflashed. Do not assume the new sync design is on either board. A
  single-board measurement is possible; the two-board sync/null test is not.
- Intended (not yet flashed) design: primary one-clock pulse every 25 µs;
  secondary `SyncTrim` on **MISC_A (PIN_65)**, 50 MHz sampler, 8-bit trim
  (20 ns ≈ 0.288° per step, default 127).
- Both use **MISC_B (PIN_60)** as UART RX; adapters TX → port B, GND common,
  adapter RX unused.
- Trim command: `0xE0 0x5A N`, recognised only between frames. Full range
  0–255 = 5.1 µs = **73.4° of carrier phase**.
- Modes (`mode_control.py`): `0xC8` normal (no modulation), `0xC9` C4,
  `0xCA` Little Star, `0xCB` 200 Hz envelope.
- Host dual sender: `demo/dual_directional_speaker.py`; use
  `--board-a-port`/`--board-b-port` for two independent links, or `--bus-port`
  if both B lines are tied (each frame written twice, ~270 ms per full update).
- The EPCS16 images on disk are the 2026-11-13 builds (primary carrier pulse
  `8e44d2e`, secondary SyncTrim `0ed0561`). They are **not on the boards**; no
  USB-Blaster is available, so plan the work around single-board measurements
  and treat any two-board sync result as blocked until JTAG is back.

## Serial-adapter warning (important)

Windows 11 was updated on 2026-11-20, and that **reinstalled the known-bad WCH
CH341 driver** (`ch341ser.inf` 3.9.2024.9, `CH341S64.SYS` 3.90, `oem277.inf`).
That release blue-screened this machine with `0x50`/`0x139` when the CH340
adapter was used. The FTDI store also contains both 2.12.28.0 and 2.12.36.20
packages; the earlier `0x139` crash came from binding a **mixed** bus/port pair.

Only one CH340 and one FTDI adapter exist. Until the driver situation is fixed,
prefer the FTDI. The CH340 should not be used for sustained streaming. If the
measurement only needs short commands (mode set, trim), a few hundred bytes at a
time is the low-risk case — but do not hot-plug the CH340 while a capture is
running.

## Physics that constrains the measurement

- λ at 40 kHz = **8.575 mm** (343 m/s, 20 °C).
- Pitch 10.5 mm > λ, so grating lobes exist; the 5.25 mm half-pitch offset
  cancels the axial **54.75°** lobe only at the nominal geometry.
- Combined aperture ≈ **331 × 163 mm**; Rayleigh distance is tens of metres, so
  1–3 m measurements are **near field**. Use `--focus-z` for spherical focusing.
- The 54.75° "null" is a **point null**. 1 mm seam/offset error raises it to
  about −23 dB; 2 mm to about −18 dB. It splits into two peaks around 52–57°
  either side of the null, which sit below the 60° boundary.
- Side leak above 60°: model says −31.3 dB with 10 mm piston directivity,
  −37.4 dB after optimizer sweeps.
- 40 kHz is inaudible; you cannot align anything by ear.

## Acceptance criteria to measure (from PLAN §1)

1. Both boards emit phase-locked carriers from the same sync source.
2. The 54.75° lobes are ≥20 dB below the main beam (target 25–30 dB after trim).
3. Main beam broadside, ≤ ±20° (target much narrower).
4. Above 60°, level ≥20 dB below the main beam.
5. Frame update atomicity documented.
6. Settings recorded and repeatable.

## The open questions this session must answer

### 1. Microphone and acquisition

What microphone actually works at 40 kHz, and how is it digitised by the scope?
Most audio measurement mics roll off at 20 kHz. Evaluate, with prices:

- 1/8" or 1/4" measurement mic with ultrasonic response (e.g. B&K 4138/4939,
  GRAS 46DP class) — likely out of budget.
- Ultrasonic MEMS mic (e.g. Knowles SPH0641LU4H-1, to ~80 kHz) + bias/preamp.
- A spare 40 kHz transducer used as a receiver (MA40S4S etc.) — narrowband but
  perfect at 40 kHz, and cheap.
- Electret capsule with known response (WM-61A etc.) — measure its roll-off
  first; may be adequate relative to a reference.
- Scope front end: AC coupling, probe attenuation, preamp gain, and whether the
  scope can average/FFT at 40 kHz. State the required bandwidth (the 195 ns
  sync pulse needs ~10 MHz; the 40 kHz carrier does not).

Deliverable: a shopping list with a recommended option and a fallback that uses
what is already on the bench.

### 2. Where is the microphone?

The measurement is only as good as the mic position relative to the array.
Propose a practical method and say what accuracy it gives:

- mechanical baseline: rail + protractor + plumb line + laser distance meter;
- camera baseline: ArUco/ChArUco markers on the array corners and on the mic,
  calibrated camera, triangulation;
- **video + VGGT** (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer): record a video of the
  scene, reconstruct camera poses and a point map. Investigate whether VGGT can
  actually place a small mic in array coordinates: scale ambiguity, need for a
  known-size marker, motion blur, and whether the mic is even segmentable. If it
  is viable, say exactly how it is calibrated; if not, say why and give the
  simpler method.
- The measurement needs mic position known to roughly **±5 mm laterally** at
  1–2 m for the 54.75° direction (≈0.3°), and the distance to ~1 %.

Also decide whether to move the mic or rotate the array/turntable, and how to
keep the array at a fixed height and orientation.

### 3. Sync verification (can be done first, with the scope only)

Probe primary **MISC_D (PIN_120)** and secondary **MISC_A (PIN_65)**:

- 25 µs period, ~195 ns pulse, no missed or doubled edges over a long capture;
- measure the static skew and confirm that trim 0→255 moves the secondary
  carrier by ~73.4° (about 5.1 µs at the carrier, i.e. the reload edge moves);
- if the pulse jitters or drops, the secondary free-runs and the pair is not
  locked — that must be fixed before any acoustic measurement.

State the scope settings (time base, trigger, averaging) and what "locked" looks
like on screen.

### 4. Room and direct-path isolation

The room is not anechoic. At 1–2 m the direct arrival is ~3–6 ms; reflections
follow. Propose how to isolate the direct field:

- time-gated capture of a short burst (what burst source? the firmware is
  continuous; ON/OFF frames give ~7.4 Hz, which is too long for gating);
- a sustained tone plus spatial averaging / near-field focusing;
- an absorber screen or an outdoor/large-room measurement;
- if a burst or coded excitation is needed, say whether it requires a firmware
  change (e.g. a gated carrier mode) and how much work that is.

Do not assume the existing 200 Hz mode is a short burst; check what it actually
does before relying on it.

### 5. Measurement sequence and metrics

Propose the exact order, with the commands and what each run proves:

1. scope sync check;
2. both boards broadside, uniform amplitude, trim 127 — reference pattern;
3. trim sweep 0–255 (step 8 first, then finer) with the mic at 54.75° —
   find the deepest null; record the value;
4. polar cuts at φ = 0°, 90°, 45°; θ = 0, 10, 20, 40, 50, 54.75, 59, 65, 70, 90°
   at the demo distance and at a second distance;
5. main-beam width (−6 dB / −20 dB) and the >60° leak;
6. repeat after 30–60 min to check thermal drift;
7. optional: taper/optimizer comparison (`--amplitude-window`, `--optimize-sweeps`).

For every capture record: mode, trim, amplitude window, mic coordinates, array
orientation, distance, temperature, time, scope settings, and the raw trace.

### 6. What would falsify success

List the failure modes up front so the data can distinguish them:

- no null at all → sync, firmware, select polarity, or wiring;
- shallow null → mechanical offset beyond ±0.15 mm, phase/amplitude mismatch,
  coplanarity;
- null far field but not near field → near-field focusing needed;
- gap fringes → smaller gap or stronger taper;
- drift → thermal asymmetry or air currents;
- two boards audibly/measurably not locked → free-run beat or moving pattern.

## Known pitfalls

- Do not trust the CH340 until the driver is fixed; a BSOD mid-capture loses the
  data and possibly the board state.
- Do not hot-plug USB adapters while a capture is running.
- The array holds its last frame after the sender exits; mute explicitly with an
  OFF frame before moving the mic.
- The null only exists at the nominal 15.75 / 5.25 geometry; a measured −20 dB
  null is still a positive result under real tolerances, but a missing null at
  −18 dB is not proof of failure until the geometry is re-measured.
- The host model is an ideal piston model; agreement within a few dB is good.
